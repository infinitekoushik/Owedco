Common questions
Does referring my client to a specialist put my CPA relationship at risk? +
No. R&D credit specialists do not do general tax work. They are brought in specifically for the credit claim and work alongside your firm. You remain your client's primary accountant. Most CPAs find that surfacing the credit strengthens the relationship because it shows you are actively looking for ways to reduce their tax burden.
What if I already told a client they don't qualify? +
It happens. The four-part test is nuanced and R&D credits are a specialised area that most general-practice CPAs are not trained in. Revisiting the question is not an admission of error. The sensible framing is "a specialist I work with can do a free review to confirm." If the specialist finds nothing, your original assessment stands.
Which industries should I be looking at in my client base? +
Software and SaaS, manufacturing, biotech and life sciences, architecture and engineering firms, food and beverage companies doing formulation work, and startups of any kind. Construction firms with in-house engineering also qualify regularly. If any of your clients have engineers, developers, scientists or technical staff solving problems that do not have standard answers, they are worth sending to the checker.
Is Owedco itself a tax credit specialist? +
No. Owedco is an information tool. We help businesses understand whether they likely qualify and connect strong matches with specialist firms. We do not calculate credits, file returns or provide tax advice. The specialists we refer to are qualified professionals who work on contingency.
How do I share Owedco with clients? +
The simplest way is to forward the link to owedco.com with a short note: "I have been looking at whether your business might qualify for the federal R&D tax credit. This free tool gives you a rough answer in 90 seconds. No email required." We also have industry-specific blog posts you can forward to clients in those sectors.
