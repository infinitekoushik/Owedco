How the Calculator Works

This calculator uses your actual expense inputs to estimate your federal R&D tax credit under IRC Section 41. You enter wages, supplies, contract research, and computing costs. The calculator applies the correct IRS treatment to each category, determines your base amount based on your method and prior year data, and applies the credit rate. No email. No form. No sales call. The number updates as you type.

Unlike most calculators you will find, this one handles the 65% contractor rule automatically, applies the 80% substantially-all wage rule when relevant, supports both ASC and Regular Credit methods with live switching, and shows state credits for 14 states stacked on top of the federal result.

ASC vs Regular Credit: Which to Use

Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC)

14% of QREs above 50% of your three-year average QREs. If no prior R&D history, 6% of current QREs with no base. The most commonly used method. Easier to document and calculate. Use this if you are claiming for the first time or do not have detailed historical data.

Regular Credit Method

20% of QREs above your base amount. The base amount is your fixed-base percentage times average gross receipts from the prior four years, with a 50% floor. More complex but can produce a higher credit for companies with low historical R&D spending relative to current revenue growth.

You can calculate both and choose whichever is higher when you file. Use the method tabs in the calculator to compare them side by side.

The 80% Substantially All Rule

If an employee spends 80% or more of their time on qualified research activities during the year, the IRS allows 100% of their wages to count as QREs. You do not have to pro-rate their wages by the actual percentage. This is called the substantially-all rule and it matters most for dedicated R&D staff who are exclusively or almost exclusively doing qualifying work. Toggle the substantially-all selector in the calculator to see the impact on your estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this calculator? ⌄
This is a preliminary estimate using the actual IRS formulas for the ASC and Regular Credit methods. The accuracy depends on how accurately you estimate your QREs and R&D time percentages. A formal credit study will produce a more precise number based on your actual payroll records, invoices, and project documentation. Use this to understand the order of magnitude before deciding whether to pursue a full study.
Can I claim credits for prior years? ⌄
Yes. You can amend returns to claim R&D credits for the prior three years. Use the year tabs in the calculator to switch between years and see what each one may be worth. The multi-year panel in the results shows your four-year total opportunity. Many first-time claimants find their back-year credits are more valuable than the current year.
What is the 65% contractor rule? ⌄
When you pay outside contractors to perform qualified research, only 65% of what you pay counts as a QRE. The IRS reduces the qualifying amount because the contractor retains some intellectual property rights. Enter the full amount you paid and the calculator applies the 65% automatically.
My business is not profitable. Can I still get the credit? ⌄
Yes, if you qualify as a startup. Enable the startup payroll offset toggle. If you have been operating for fewer than five years and have under $5 million in gross receipts, you can apply up to $500,000 of the credit against your employer payroll tax liability each year. This converts the credit into real cash savings even with no income tax to offset.
What is the Section 280C election and should I use it? ⌄
When you claim the R&D credit, the IRS requires you to reduce your wage deduction by the credit amount. The Section 280C(c)(2) election lets you take a reduced credit rate instead, so you keep the full wage deduction. At a 21% corporate tax rate, the reduced credit is roughly 11% under ASC instead of 14%. For some businesses this simplifies the tax return. Toggle it in the calculator to compare both scenarios for your numbers.
Do I need a specialist to claim the credit? ⌄
Not required, but strongly recommended for claims above $20,000. The calculation is straightforward but the documentation requirements, the four-part test applied to your specific activities, and audit risk all benefit from a specialist. Fees are typically 20-30% of the credit recovered, with no fee if no credit is found. For a $100,000 credit, paying $20,000-$30,000 to a specialist to find and defend it is usually the right economic call.
