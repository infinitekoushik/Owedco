How the Calculator Works

Enter your qualified research expenses across four categories: wages, supplies, contract research, and computing costs. The calculator applies the correct IRS treatment to each one, works out your base amount using your chosen method and prior year data, then applies the credit rate. The number updates as you type. No email, no form, no gated result.

Most calculators online take wages only and show a rough range. This one handles the 65 percent contractor rule automatically, applies the 80 percent substantially-all wage rule when it applies, supports both ASC and Regular Credit methods with live switching, shows state credits for 20 states on top of the federal result, and calculates your four-year recoverable total so you know what prior year amendments are worth before you pick up the phone.

ASC vs Regular Credit: Which to Use

Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC)

14 percent of QREs above 50 percent of your three-year average QREs. No prior R&D history? You get 6 percent of current QREs with no base amount reduction. The most commonly used method because it is easier to document and calculate. Good choice for first-time claimants and businesses without detailed historical data.

Regular Credit Method

20 percent of QREs above your base amount. The base amount uses your fixed-base percentage times your average gross receipts from the prior four years, with a 50 percent floor. More complex but can produce a higher credit for companies with low historical R&D spending relative to current revenue growth. Worth running both to compare.

You can run both methods and pick whichever is higher when you file. Use the method tabs in the calculator to switch between them and see the difference on your numbers.

The 80 Percent Substantially All Rule

When an employee spends 80 percent or more of their time on qualified research activities during the year, the IRS allows 100 percent of their wages to count as QREs. No pro-rating required. The substantially-all rule matters most for dedicated R&D staff who spend almost all of their working hours on qualifying work. Toggle the selector in the calculator to see how much it changes your estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this calculator? ⌄
This is a preliminary estimate using the actual IRS formulas for the ASC and Regular Credit methods. The number depends on how accurately you estimate your QREs and R&D time percentages. A formal credit study will produce a more precise figure based on your actual payroll records, invoices, and project documentation. Use this to understand the order of magnitude before deciding whether to pursue a full study.
Can I claim credits for prior years? ⌄
Yes. You can amend returns to claim R&D credits for the prior three years. Use the year tabs in the calculator to switch between years and see what each one may be worth. The multi-year panel in the results shows your four-year total. Many first-time claimants find their back-year credits are worth more than the current year because their R&D spending was already happening, they just were not tracking it.
What is the 65 percent contractor rule? ⌄
When you pay outside contractors to perform qualified research, only 65 percent of what you pay counts as a QRE. The IRS reduces the qualifying amount because the contractor retains some intellectual property rights and does not bear the full financial risk of the research. Enter the full contract amount and the calculator applies the 65 percent rule automatically.
My business is not profitable. Can I still benefit? ⌄
Yes, if you qualify as a startup. Enable the startup payroll offset toggle. If you have been operating for fewer than five years and have under $5 million in gross receipts, you can apply up to $500,000 of the credit against your employer payroll tax liability each year. No income tax required. The credit comes off your quarterly payroll tax payments instead.
What is the Section 280C election? ⌄
When you claim the R&D credit, the IRS requires you to reduce your wage deduction by the credit amount. The Section 280C(c)(2) election lets you take a reduced credit rate instead, so you keep the full wage deduction. At a 21 percent corporate tax rate, the reduced credit is roughly 11 percent under ASC instead of 14 percent. Toggle it in the calculator to compare both scenarios on your actual numbers.
Do I need a specialist to claim the credit? ⌄
Not required, but strongly recommended for claims above $20,000. The calculation is not complicated but the documentation requirements, the four-part test applied to your specific activities, and audit risk all benefit from someone who does this work full time. Fees are typically 20 to 30 percent of the credit recovered, with no fee if no credit is found. For a $100,000 credit, paying a specialist to find and defend it is usually the right call.
