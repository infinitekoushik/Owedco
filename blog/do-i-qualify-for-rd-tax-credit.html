Do I Qualify for the R&D Tax Credit?

Owedco  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  9 min read + free checklist

Most businesses that qualify have no idea they do. The IRS definition of "research" is much broader than most people assume and the businesses that miss out are usually the ones that never bothered to check.

Quick Eligibility Check: 4 Questions
1. Does your business develop or improve any product, process, software or technique?
2. Does that work involve any technical uncertainty: problems your team had to figure out?
3. Do you employ people (W-2 or contractors) who spend time on that development work?
4. Is the work done in the United States?

The IRS Four-Part Test: What Actually Determines Eligibility

The R&D tax credit under IRC Section 41 uses a specific four-part test. Every activity you want to claim must pass all four. Here is what each part actually means, with real examples of what passes and what does not.

1
Permitted Purpose
The work must be aimed at developing or improving a product, process, software, technique, formula or invention. The improvement needs to relate to function, performance, reliability or quality, not just aesthetics.
Passes: Building a new feature, improving a manufacturing process, developing a drug compound, redesigning a component to reduce failure rate
Fails: Repainting a product, changing a logo, market research, customer surveys
2
Technological in Nature
The work must rely on hard science: computer science, engineering, biology, chemistry or physics. It does not need to be cutting-edge. It just needs to apply scientific principles.
Passes: Software development, mechanical engineering, chemical formulation, electrical design, materials testing
Fails: Marketing copy, financial modelling, social science research, management consulting
3
Elimination of Technical Uncertainty
You must be trying to discover information that eliminates uncertainty about whether, or how, you can develop or improve something. The uncertainty must be technical, not commercial or business uncertainty.
Passes: "Can we build this to handle 100k users?" / "Will this compound bind to the target receptor?" / "Can we hit this tolerance with this alloy?"
Fails: "Will customers buy this?" / "Is this market big enough?" / "Which colour converts better?"
4
Process of Experimentation
You must be evaluating alternatives through a process of experimentation: testing hypotheses, trying different approaches, iterating. You do not need a formal lab. Jira tickets, GitHub branches, engineering notes and prototype iterations all count.
Passes: Testing different architectures, trying multiple formulations, running production trials, building and abandoning approaches
Fails: Following a known recipe exactly, executing a documented process with no iteration
The key insight most businesses miss

You do not need to succeed. A failed experiment, a prototype that did not work, a feature you abandoned, an approach that proved unviable: that is still qualified research. The IRS credits the process of experimentation, not the outcome.

Does Your Industry Qualify?

The credit has no industry restriction. What matters is the nature of the work, not what sector you are in. That said, some industries qualify almost universally while others rarely do.

What You Actually Need to Qualify

Strip it down and you need three things:

  1. Qualifying activity: development or improvement work that involves technical uncertainty and experimentation
  2. US-based employees or contractors: someone whose wages or fees you can tie to that activity
  3. Some form of documentation: records that show what you were trying to do and how you went about it

That is it. No lab required. No PhD required. No minimum company size. No minimum revenue. Sole proprietors qualify. Pre-revenue startups qualify. Businesses that have never heard of the credit qualify.

What about company size?

There is no minimum. A 2-person software company can claim the credit. There is a restriction at the top end: businesses with over $50M in average gross receipts over the prior 3 years cannot use the credit to offset AMT, but they can still claim it against regular income tax.

How Much Could You Get?

The credit runs at roughly 6 to 14% of your qualifying R&D expenses, which include wages, 65% of contractor costs and supplies consumed in research. For a software company with $500k in engineering payroll where 70% of time is qualifying work, that is $350k in QREs and a credit of roughly $21,000 to $49,000 per year.
