How the R&D Tax Credit Is Calculated

There are two IRS-approved methods. This calculator uses both and shows you which one produces the larger credit.

Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC)

The ASC gives you 14% of the QREs that exceed 50% of your average QREs over the prior 3 years. If you have no prior-year QREs at all (first year or startup), the rate is a flat 6% of all current-year QREs. This is the method most small businesses use because it requires less historical data.

Regular Credit Method (RC)

The RC gives you 20% of QREs above a calculated base amount. The base uses your average R&D intensity from a fixed historical period. For businesses that spent very little on R&D in prior years but are spending heavily now, this often produces a larger credit than the ASC. Specialists always model both and file whichever is higher.

QRE stands for Qualified Research Expenses. It is the sum of your qualifying wages, 65% of contractor costs and qualifying supplies. That is what the calculator builds from your inputs.

Startups: The Payroll Tax Offset Changes the Math Entirely

If your business is under 5 years old with under $5M in gross receipts, you can apply up to $500,000 per year of your R&D credit directly against employer payroll taxes. That is real cash out of your quarterly 941 payments, even if you have never turned a profit.

The calculator shows you this separately when you select Startup. Even a $30,000 credit is $30,000 off your next payroll tax bill, not a future benefit you have to wait on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I calculate the credit myself and file it without a specialist?

Yes. Form 6765 is the IRS form for the R&D credit and you or your CPA can complete it. The risk is underclaiming, miscalculating the base amount or creating audit exposure through insufficient documentation. For credits over $25,000 a specialist study is generally worth it.

Is this the same as the Section 174 deduction?

No. The Section 41 credit (what this calculator estimates) reduces your tax dollar-for-dollar. The Section 174 deduction reduces your taxable income. They work differently and can sometimes be used together. See our credit vs deduction guide for the full breakdown.

What if my estimate seems too high?

Double-check that you are entering only the portion of wages spent on qualifying R&D, not total payroll. An engineer who spends 40% of their time on development contributes 40% of their wages to your QRE, not 100%.

Can I claim prior years I missed?

Yes. You can amend tax returns for the prior 3 years. If you have been doing qualifying work for years without claiming the credit, your first recovery often stacks multiple years together.
