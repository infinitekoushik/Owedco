R&D Tax Credit vs Section 179: Which One Wins for Your Business (2026)

These two tax tools are not competing with each other. They do completely different things. But every year business owners pick one, ignore the other, and leave money on the table. Here is the actual difference, when each one applies, and why the right answer for most businesses is both.

What Each One Actually Does

This is where most people get confused. A deduction and a credit are not the same thing, and that difference matters enormously when you are doing the math.

A deduction reduces your taxable income. If you are in a 25% tax bracket and you take a $100,000 deduction, you save $25,000 in taxes. The deduction is worth a fraction of its face value depending on your tax rate.

A credit reduces your tax bill directly. If you have a $25,000 R&D tax credit, that $25,000 comes off what you owe the IRS after all the other math is done. Dollar for dollar. No bracket math required.

Section 179 is a deduction. The R&D tax credit is a credit. They are fundamentally different animals.

The Situations Where Each One Wins

You bought $300,000 of new manufacturing equipment this year

Section 179 lets you deduct the full $300,000 in year one instead of depreciating it over 5-7 years. At a 25% tax rate that is $75,000 in tax savings this year instead of waiting. The R&D credit does not cover equipment purchases unless that equipment was built for research purposes and consumed in the research process.

Use Section 179 here.
You spent $400,000 in engineer wages developing a new product

Section 179 does not touch wages. The R&D tax credit was built exactly for this. At 14% ASC, that is potentially $56,000 coming off your tax bill directly. You cannot deduct wages under Section 179 no matter how you structure it.

Use R&D credit here.
You built custom R&D equipment specifically to use in your research process

This is where it gets interesting. If you designed and built equipment for research purposes and it was consumed in or used directly in research, those costs can qualify as R&D supplies. You may also be able to take Section 179 on that same equipment. Talk to a specialist before you file because the interaction between these two is nuanced.

Potentially both. Get a specialist to run the numbers.
You are pre-revenue with no taxable income

Section 179 is useless here. You cannot take a deduction larger than your business income, and if you have no income there is nothing to deduct against. The R&D startup payroll offset lets you apply up to $500,000 of R&D credits against your payroll tax liability instead. Real money out of your pocket, no profit required.

R&D credit wins by default.
You are profitable and doing both equipment upgrades and product development

Take Section 179 on the equipment. Claim the R&D credit on the qualifying research wages and supplies. These two do not conflict with each other in most situations. You file Form 4562 for Section 179 and Form 6765 for the R&D credit. Both in the same tax return, same year.

Take both. This is the right answer for most established businesses.

The Question People Always Ask: Can I Take Both?

Usually yes, with one catch. If you claim the R&D tax credit on wages and supplies used in research, you have to reduce your deduction for those same expenses by the amount of the credit. This is called the credit reduction rule under IRC Section 280C.

Here is what that looks like in practice. You spend $200,000 on R&D wages. You claim a $28,000 R&D credit (14% ASC). You then have to reduce your wage deduction by $28,000, so you can only deduct $172,000 as wages instead of the full $200,000. At a 25% tax rate, that reduction costs you $7,000 in additional taxes. The credit still nets you $21,000 ahead. The math almost always favors taking the credit.

There is an election under Section 280C(c)(2) to take a reduced credit rate instead of reducing your deductions. The reduced credit is your marginal rate times the normal credit. At 21% corporate rate that gives you roughly 11% instead of 14%. For some businesses this election makes sense. A specialist can run both scenarios in about ten minutes.

Short version: Yes you can take both. The interaction reduces the benefit slightly but the combined position almost always beats either one alone.

What Section 179 Cannot Do That the R&D Credit Can

Section 179 is a great tool for equipment-heavy businesses. But it has hard limits that the R&D credit does not.

You cannot take Section 179 on wages. Full stop. If your biggest cost is people doing development work, Section 179 does nothing for that spending. The R&D credit was specifically designed to reward investment in people doing technical work.

You cannot carry Section 179 forward. If you have a loss year or a low-income year, unused Section 179 disappears. R&D credits carry forward for 20 years. A startup that spends years burning through investment capital before turning profitable can accumulate credits the entire time and apply them once revenue kicks in.

Section 179 phases out if you spend more than $2.89 million on property in 2026. Large capital-intensive businesses hit this ceiling and lose the benefit. There is no equivalent phase-out for the R&D credit.

What the R&D Credit Cannot Do That Section 179 Can

The R&D credit will not help you with a straightforward equipment purchase. Buying a CNC machine, a fleet of trucks, or a new HVAC system for your facility is not research. Section 179 handles that immediately and simply. You buy it, you deduct it, you move on.

Section 179 also has lower documentation requirements. You need purchase records and an asset log. The R&D credit requires you to document which activities qualify, which employees did them, what percentage of their time was spent on qualifying work, and which supplies were consumed in research. That is more work, and for very small businesses with minimal R&D activity, the compliance cost can eat into the benefit.

Do not skip the R&D credit just because Section 179 is easier to claim. For any business with meaningful technical development work, the R&D credit delivers far more value per dollar than a deduction will.

The Right Way to Think About This in 2026

Tax planning is not about picking the single best tool. It is about using every tool you are legally entitled to. The businesses that maximize their after-tax cash flow are the ones that treat tax strategy the same way they treat pricing strategy or hiring strategy. They do the work.

If you are running a business that buys equipment and does product or process development work, you probably have two separate conversations to have with your tax advisor before you file. One about Section 179 on your capital purchases. One about R&D credits on your development activity. They are separate forms, separate calculations, and separate conversations.

Most general CPAs are comfortable with Section 179. Many are not deeply familiar with the nuances of R&D credit documentation requirements, the four-part test, or the distinction between qualifying and non-qualifying research. If your CPA has never proactively mentioned the R&D credit to you, that is a gap worth filling before April.
