R&D Tax Credit for Food and Beverage Companies (2026 Guide)

You are developing new recipes, reformulating products to hit clean-label requirements, engineering your production line for better shelf life, and running food safety trials that cost real money. The IRS will give you a percentage of those costs back. Most food and beverage companies do not know this. The ones that do are pocketing six figures a year in credits.

$25,000 - $250,000+
Typical annual R&D tax credit range for a mid-size food and beverage manufacturer claiming qualified research expenses for the first time.

Why Food and Beverage Qualifies

There is a persistent myth that R&D tax credits are for tech companies in lab coats. That myth is costing food and beverage business owners money every single year.

The federal R&D tax credit under IRC Section 41 covers any business that conducts "qualified research." The IRS defines that as research that is technological in nature, intended to discover information that eliminates uncertainty, and conducted through a process of experimentation. Food and beverage development hits all three of those tests constantly.

When you are trying to extend shelf life without synthetic preservatives, you are eliminating technical uncertainty. When you run a dozen iterations of a sauce formulation to nail viscosity, you are conducting a process of experimentation. When you build a new fermentation protocol from scratch, that is technological in nature. The IRS does not require you to be a pharmaceutical company to claim the credit.

What Qualifies: The Activity Breakdown

Qualifies

  • New recipe and formula development
  • Reformulating to remove allergens or artificial ingredients
  • Shelf life extension testing and trials
  • Food safety protocol development (HACCP, pathogen control)
  • New processing or manufacturing method development
  • Packaging innovation (seal integrity, modified atmosphere)
  • Scale-up testing from lab batch to production batch
  • Flavor stability and sensory testing under varying conditions
  • Texture or mouthfeel engineering (emulsification, hydrocolloid work)
  • Nutritional profile optimization and fortification trials
  • Fermentation process development
  • Custom equipment design or adaptation for production
  • Plant-based or alternative protein formulation
  • Developing proprietary blends, spice mixes, or concentrates

Does Not Qualify

  • Adapting an existing recipe for a new flavor (color swap, minor tweak)
  • Quality control testing of existing processes
  • Marketing research or consumer surveys
  • Style, taste preference, or aesthetic changes with no technical uncertainty
  • Production of food for sale (routine manufacturing)
  • Management or business process work
  • Social science or market demand research
  • Complying with existing FDA labeling requirements (no technical experiment involved)
The line that matters: If your team already knows how to do it and is just doing it again, it does not qualify. If your team is running trials because the outcome is uncertain, it very likely does.

What Costs You Can Claim

The R&D tax credit is based on your Qualified Research Expenses (QREs). In food and beverage, these typically include:

Employee Wages

This is usually the biggest bucket. If your food scientists, R&D chefs, process engineers, or production supervisors spend time on qualifying research activities, the wages for that time count. You need to track what percentage of their time is spent on R&D versus routine production. Even if it is 20% of one person's time, that portion is claimable.

Supplies Consumed in Research

Ingredients used in trial batches that are not sold, lab materials, test packaging runs, pilot-scale production batches that are part of a development process. These are qualified supply expenses. If you ran 15 iterations of a formula before settling on the right one, those 15 batches of ingredients are claimable.

Contract Research

Paid a food science lab to run safety tests? Hired a flavor house to develop a proprietary extract? Used a co-manufacturer to run pilot batches? 65% of what you paid them counts as a QRE if the work would otherwise qualify as research you conducted yourself.

Computer Costs

Software or computing time used directly in research, such as simulation software for process modeling or analytical lab software, qualifies at the same rate as supply expenses.

Typical QRE breakdown for a food company: 60-70% wages, 20-30% supplies (trial batch ingredients), 10-15% contract research. The mix shifts toward supplies if you run a lot of pilot production.

How Much Credit Can You Get

The federal credit is calculated two ways. Most food and beverage companies use the Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC) because it is easier to document.

Under ASC, the credit is 14% of QREs that exceed 50% of your average QREs from the prior three years. If you are just starting to track R&D expenses and have no base period, you get 6% of current-year QREs with no baseline requirement.

Example: A specialty food manufacturer with $500,000 in annual QREs and a $300,000 three-year average would calculate the credit on $500,000 minus $150,000 (50% of average), which is $350,000. At 14%, that is a $49,000 federal credit. That is real money coming off your actual tax bill, not your taxable income.

Startups and pre-revenue companies: if you have less than $5 million in gross receipts and have been operating for fewer than five years, you can apply up to $500,000 of the credit against payroll taxes instead of income tax. You do not need to be profitable to benefit.

Industries Within Food and Beverage That Claim the Most

Craft Breweries and Distilleries

New yeast strain development, fermentation protocol design, barrel aging experiments, new spirit formulations. Craft producers often have more qualifying activity per dollar of revenue than large beverage companies because nearly everything they do is experimental by nature.

Specialty Food Manufacturers

Clean-label reformulation is one of the richest areas right now. Removing artificial preservatives, sweeteners, or emulsifiers while maintaining product performance requires genuine technical experimentation. If you have done this in the last three years, you have unclaimed credits sitting on the table.

Plant-Based and Alternative Protein Producers

This category is almost entirely R&D. Binding agents, texture modification, flavor masking, color stability, moisture retention in meat alternatives. If you are building products in this space, your entire product development budget is potentially claimable.

Sauce, Condiment, and Ingredient Manufacturers

Shelf-stable emulsion development, viscosity engineering, natural color stability, high-pressure processing trials. These are technical problems with uncertain outcomes and they qualify.

Baked Goods and Snack Companies

Gluten-free formulation, reduced-fat texture work, novel extrusion processes, clean-label leavening systems. The baking industry has significant R&D activity that routinely goes unclaimed.

Nutraceuticals and Functional Foods

Bioavailability testing, encapsulation technology, stability of active ingredients under processing and storage. This sits at the intersection of food science and supplement development. Almost every product iteration qualifies.

Documentation: What the IRS Wants to See

You do not need a formal lab with notebooks to claim this credit. But you do need to be able to show that qualified research happened. Here is what good documentation looks like for food and beverage companies:

  • Product development logs or trial batch records (dates, ingredients, iterations, outcomes)
  • Sensory evaluation records showing rounds of testing
  • Shelf life study data and timelines
  • Internal emails, Slack messages, or meeting notes discussing technical problems and solutions
  • Payroll records that can be tied to R&D project time
  • Vendor invoices for ingredients used in trial batches
  • Lab results from third-party food safety or nutritional testing
  • Failed batch records (proving you were experimenting, not just producing)
Failed batches are your best evidence. If you threw out 10 test batches before getting the formula right, that is proof of a process of experimentation. Do not hide the failures. Document them.

Common Mistakes Food Companies Make When Claiming

Only Claiming Lab Staff

Your R&D chef is not the only person doing qualifying work. Production supervisors who manage trial runs, quality assurance staff who develop new testing protocols, and even administrative staff tracking project costs can have portions of their wages included. Scope the claim correctly.

Forgetting Trial Batch Ingredients

Ingredients consumed in test production are QREs. Most food companies track these poorly because they run through the same inventory system as production ingredients. Separate your trial batches from production batches in your inventory tracking and you will find a significant expense category you were missing.

Claiming the Credit for the Wrong Years

You can amend up to three prior years of tax returns to claim R&D credits you missed. If you were developing new products in 2023, 2024, and 2025 and did not claim, you can go back and get those credits. This is often the most valuable conversation a first-time claimant has with a specialist.

Giving Up Because You Are Not Profitable

Pre-revenue food companies and early-stage manufacturers can apply the credit against payroll taxes. The startup payroll offset is specifically designed for this situation. Unprofitable does not mean ineligible.

State R&D Credits for Food and Beverage

Fourteen states offer their own R&D tax credits that stack on top of the federal credit. If you manufacture in California, New York, Texas, Illinois, or Georgia, there is a state credit that runs parallel to your federal claim. Some states are particularly generous for food and ag-tech businesses.

California's credit is 15% of QREs above the base amount and has no cap. New York's credit is 9% of QREs. These are not small numbers when your annual QREs are in the hundreds of thousands. Use our R&D credit calculator to estimate both federal and state amounts for your situation.

The Bottom Line

Food and beverage is one of the most underserved industries when it comes to R&D tax credit claims. The IRS does not advertise this. The CPAs who do general business taxes often do not know the specifics. And so year after year, food companies doing legitimate, documented research development work file their taxes and leave money in Washington.

The federal credit is worth up to 20% of your qualified research expenses. Your state may add another 9-15% on top. If you have been spending $200,000 a year on product development and never claimed, you may have $80,000 to $120,000 sitting in prior year returns that you can still recover.
