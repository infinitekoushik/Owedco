R&D Tax Credit vs R&D Deduction: Which Is Worth More in 2026?

By Owedco Research Team  ·  Updated September 2026  ·  10 min read

Most small business owners have heard of the R&D tax credit. Fewer know there's also an R&D deduction and that Congress has been fighting over it for years. Even fewer know that you can often use both or that the right combination depends entirely on your tax situation.

Here's the plain-English breakdown: what each one is, how they're calculated, what changed in 2022 and how to figure out which strategy puts more money back in your pocket.

R&D Tax Credit

IRC Section 41

  • Dollar-for-dollar reduction in tax owed
  • Based on qualifying research expenses
  • Carries forward 20 years if unused
  • Can offset payroll tax (startups)
  • Reduces your tax bill directly
R&D Deduction

IRC Section 174

  • Reduces taxable income (not tax)
  • Broader set of qualifying expenses
  • Now requires 5-year amortization (US)
  • 15-year amortization for foreign R&D
  • Contested in Congress since 2022

The Core Difference: Credits vs. Deductions

A deduction reduces your taxable income. If you're in the 21% corporate tax bracket and you deduct $100,000 of R&D expenses, you save $21,000 in taxes.

A credit reduces your tax bill directly. A $21,000 R&D credit saves you $21,000 regardless of your tax bracket. Credits are almost always more valuable than deductions of the same dollar amount.

The Simple Rule

A $1 credit is worth more than a $1 deduction. A credit at the 21% tax rate is mathematically equivalent to a deduction of about $4.76. If you can get the credit, get the credit.

What the Section 174 Change Did (And Why It Still Matters in 2026)

Before 2022, Section 174 let businesses immediately deduct 100% of R&D expenses in the year they occurred. Starting with the 2022 tax year, domestic R&D expenses must be amortized over 5 years (15 years for foreign R&D).

Pre-2022

Section 174 allowed 100% immediate expensing of R&D costs. Most small businesses had been doing this for decades.

2022 — The Surprise Change

TCJA provision kicks in. Domestic R&D must now be amortized over 5 years. Many businesses owed unexpected taxes because they could only deduct 10% of their annual R&D spend in year 1.

2023–2025 — Congressional Gridlock

Multiple bills proposed to restore immediate expensing. None passed into law.

2026 — Current Status

Section 174 amortization remains in effect. Consult a tax advisor for the latest — this is actively contested territory.

⚠️ Check With Your Tax Advisor on Current 174 Status

The Section 174 rules have been subject to ongoing legislative proposals. The status as of your filing date may differ from what's described here.

Can You Take Both the Credit and the Deduction?

Yes but there's an adjustment. If you claim the Section 41 R&D tax credit, you must reduce your Section 174 deduction by the amount of the credit. You can avoid this by electing the "reduced credit" under IRC Section 280C, which lets you keep your full deduction at a slightly lower credit rate.

The 280C Election: When It Helps

If you're a profitable business in a high tax bracket, the reduced credit election (keeping your full deduction) often produces a better combined outcome. Your tax advisor should model both scenarios before you file.

Real Example: $500k in R&D Spending, Two Scenarios

Scenario A — Credit Only (Full Rate)

Section 41 ASC Credit (14% × $500k):+$70,000
Section 174 Deduction Reduction ($70k × 21%):−$14,700
Net Tax Benefit:$55,300

Scenario B — Reduced Credit Election (280C)

Reduced ASC Credit (~12.1% × $500k):+$60,500
Full Section 174 Deduction retained (21%):+$21,000*
Net Tax Benefit:$81,500

*Illustrative only — not tax advice. Actual benefit depends on your full tax situation.

Verdict on This Example

Scenario B produces roughly $26,000 more in net tax benefit for a profitable company at 21%. But this flips for companies with low taxable income or those using the payroll tax offset. Always model both.

Which Strategy Is Right for Your Business?

Use the full R&D credit (no 280C election) if:

  • You're pre-revenue or pre-profit and want to offset payroll taxes
  • Your effective tax rate is low (under ~18%), making the deduction less valuable
  • You have minimal Section 174 expenses and the deduction reduction is small

Use the reduced credit election (280C) if:

  • You're a profitable business with a meaningful tax rate (21%+)
  • You have large R&D expenses and the deduction is worth keeping

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming your CPA has already claimed this. Most general-practice CPAs don't specialise in R&D credits. Ask explicitly.
  • Thinking you need a profit to benefit. Startups can use the payroll offset.
  • Claiming the credit without a proper study. An undocumented credit creates audit risk without a defensible position.
  • Missing retroactive claims. You can amend returns for the prior 3 years.
  • Not modeling the 280C election. A few hours of analysis can be worth tens of thousands of dollars.
